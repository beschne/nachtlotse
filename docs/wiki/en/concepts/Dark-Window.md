# Dark Window

**Deutsch:** [Dunkelphase](Dunkelphase)

The dark window is the part of the night Nachtlotse plans for. Normally it
runs from the end of astronomical twilight in the evening to its start in the
morning, the time when the Sun is more than 18° below the horizon.

For a date, Nachtlotse uses the night that starts on the evening of that
date. If you plan for "tonight" while it's already dark, it uses the night
you're in.

## Nights without astronomical darkness

Around midsummer the Sun doesn't get 18° below the horizon in central
Europe. At the Volkssternwarte Hochtaunus (50.2°N) this is the case from
May 30 to July 13. On such nights Nachtlotse uses nautical twilight instead
(Sun more than 12° below the horizon). On June 21 that window runs from
23:32 to 03:23 local time.

Such a night is clearly marked: "nautical only, no astronomical darkness" in
the header, and every verdict is capped at MARGINAL because the sky stays
brighter.

Far in the north the Sun doesn't even get 12° below the horizon in summer.
For those nights Nachtlotse reports that there is no dark window, instead of
showing a plan.

The edges of the season are a special case. On May 29 and July 14 there is
still a short astronomical window at 50°N (17 and 49 minutes), and that is
what gets used.
